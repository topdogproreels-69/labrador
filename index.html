Training advice that actually fits your Labrador

Your Labrador is friendly, eager, strong and food-motivated, and was bred to retrieve all day, in and out of the water. Generic training tips often skip the things Lab owners care about most: a dog who would do anything for food (including stealing it), a big, happy greeter who knocks people over, a retriever who runs off with socks and won’t give the ball back, and an adolescence that can seem to last forever.

How to Train Your Labrador Retriever is a reward-based training ebook written around this one breed. It takes you from the first day of training through a 30-day plan, with short daily sessions and printable checklists.

  • Module 1, Why Breed Matters: a water dog’s history, a retriever’s drive, a big appetite and how a Labrador learns, plus a profile of your dog.
  • Module 2, Foundations: rewards (without the extra pounds), markers, timing and short sessions.

How we train

Reward-based methods only. No choke, prong, shock or bark collars, no leash jerks, no punishment.

For guarding, aggression, fear or medical concerns, see your veterinarian first, then a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist. The book shows you how to prepare for those visits.

Who it's for

  • New Labrador puppy owners who want to start right
  • Owners of adolescent or adult Labs who jump up, steal food, pull on the leash, mouth, or won’t give the ball back
  • Families who want kind, consistent rules everyone can follow

Not for: anyone looking for punishment-based or “dominance” methods, as a replacement for professional help with serious aggression or guarding, or as a gundog, field-trial or hunt-test training manual.

Is this only for puppies?

No. It works for puppies, adolescents and adults. Puppy tips appear throughout the lessons, Lesson 6.5 covers puppy mouthing and nipping, and the first-week guide and milestone tracker are for puppies.

My Lab is obsessed with food. Will this help?

It gives you a plan: tiny rewards taken from the daily food, waiting for the bowl, leave it, and counter-surfing and scavenging prevention (Lessons 2.1, 3.5 and 5.2). Decisions about diet and your dog’s weight belong to your vet.

Does it work for black, yellow and chocolate Labs?

Yes. Color doesn’t change how a Lab learns. Lesson 1.3 covers size and energy, and Lesson 5.7 covers coat, water and ear care for every color.

My Lab guards food, snaps or growls at people. Will this fix it?

This book is not a substitute for professional help. Please see your veterinarian first, then a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist. The book includes safe management and prevention habits, plus a visit prep kit to help you work with them.
